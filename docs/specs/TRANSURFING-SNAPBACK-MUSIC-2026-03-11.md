# 🎧 Snap Back: The Music Was Always Yours

### A Transurfing Visualization for Joey

---

*Read slowly. Let each line land before moving to the next. This isn't something you force — it's something you fall into.*

---

## The Grip

I notice it first in my chest. That tightness. The one that shows up every time I think about making music — the one that says *this matters too much.* The one that turns a simple idea into a mountain. I've been white-knuckling this thing for years. Holding it so close to my face I can't even see it anymore.

I notice how I've made it enormous. How I turned "I want to make a beat" into "I need to prove something." How I let every unfinished session become evidence. How a disagreement with Brooke could spiral because everything felt heavy — because I was carrying the weight of all the things I hadn't done yet, and it leaked into everything.

I notice the pendulum. That voice — *it's too hard, you don't know enough, real producers started younger, the process is overwhelming* — and I watch it swing. Back and forth. I've been feeding it for years. Giving it my attention. Letting it convince me that the gap between where I am and where I want to be is some impossible canyon.

I see it clearly now.

And I let go of the rope.

---

## The Release

I don't fight the pendulum. I don't argue with it. I don't try to convince myself I'm wrong for feeling stuck. I just… stop holding on.

*Drop it. Forget it. Wake up lighter.*

I feel my hands open. Not metaphorically — actually. My shoulders drop two inches. My jaw unclenches. Something in my ribcage loosens, like a knot I didn't know was there just came undone.

The importance drains out of it. Not the desire — the *importance.* That's the difference. The desire is clean. The desire is just energy that wants to move. The importance is the thing that blocks it. The story that says *if I can't do this, something is wrong with me.*

Nothing is wrong with me.

I just made it too big. And the bigger I made it, the further it floated away. That's how it works. Grip tighter, lose faster.

So I let it be small. I let it be light. I let it be *fun.*

---

## The Slide

I'm sitting in my chair. Headphones on. FL Studio is open — the familiar grid, the dark interface, the glow of the screen the only light in the room. Chicago is quiet outside. It's late, or early. Doesn't matter. Boo is curled up somewhere behind me. I can hear Teddy padding across the floor in the other room.

I'm not thinking about making a hit. I'm not thinking about process. I'm just… clicking around.

A kick drum. I audition three, pick one that hits low and warm. Not perfect. Just right enough. I drop it on the grid.

A hi-hat. That crisp, tight tick — the one that makes Kaytranada beats feel like they're breathing. I nudge it slightly off-grid. Just a touch. The groove shows up immediately. It's not something I calculated. My hand just knew where to put it.

I add a bass line. Something round, something that sits in your chest. Two notes. That's all it needs. The simplicity doesn't scare me — it excites me. I've stopped confusing complexity with quality.

A Rhodes sample floats in. Warm, slightly detuned, like a memory of a song you heard in a dream. I pitch it down. Layer a vinyl crackle underneath. The whole thing starts breathing.

I'm nodding my head. Not because I'm trying to — because I can't help it.

This is the part I never let myself get to. This is the part that was always on the other side of the grip. The moment where the track starts talking back to you. Where you stop building it and start *following* it.

I add a vocal chop — just a syllable, pitched and stretched until it doesn't sound human anymore, just *felt.* It sits in the pocket like it was always there. I'm smiling. Actually smiling, alone in this room, headphones on, because the thing coming out of these speakers is *mine* and it sounds like something Mac would've vibed to. Something Drake would hear and go quiet for a second before nodding.

It's not perfect. I don't need it to be perfect. That was the old trap — perfection as a prerequisite for permission. I'm done with that.

The beat is maybe two minutes long. It loops. I listen to it four times in a row without touching anything. Each pass, I like it more. Not because I'm adding to it — because I'm *hearing* it. Actually hearing what's there instead of obsessing over what's missing.

Brooke walks in. She doesn't say anything at first — just leans against the doorframe, listening. Then: "Wait, you made that?" And she's not performing surprise. She's genuinely caught off guard. Because the thing playing through the monitors sounds like *something.* Something real.

I pull one headphone off and look at her and I'm just… easy. Not proving anything. Not desperate for validation. Just a guy who made a beat he likes, and his girl heard it, and that's enough.

"Yeah," I say. "Just messing around."

But we both know it's more than that.

---

## The Door

Here's the thing I finally understand: the door was never locked. There was no gatekeeper. No threshold of skill I had to reach before I was *allowed.* I was standing in front of an open door, staring at it, making lists of reasons I wasn't ready to walk through it.

The music didn't need me to be ready. It needed me to be *light.* To stop treating it like a test and start treating it like play. The path of least resistance isn't laziness — it's alignment. It's what happens when you stop pushing against your own current.

I see the Rancho Mirage studio. Not as a fantasy — as a continuation. The desert light coming through floor-to-ceiling windows. Monitors on a walnut desk. The pool visible through the glass. The same feeling I have right now in Chicago, just with more space around it. The music didn't start there. It started *here.* In this room. On this night. The moment I decided it was allowed to be easy.

---

## The Anchor

I press my fingertips together — thumb to middle finger, both hands. I hold it for three seconds while the beat plays in my mind. The hi-hat tick. The bass in my chest. The glow of the screen. Brooke in the doorway. The ease.

This is my anchor. Any time I feel the grip coming back — the tightness, the importance, the pendulum — I press my fingers together and I'm back here. In the room where I remembered that the music was never the hard part.

*The letting go was the hard part.*

And I already did it.
