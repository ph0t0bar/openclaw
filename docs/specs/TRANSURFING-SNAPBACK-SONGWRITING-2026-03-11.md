# ✍️ Snap Back: The Words Were Always Yours

### A Transurfing Visualization for Joey

---

*Read this one lighter than the last. This one already happened. You're just writing it down.*

---

## The Moment

It's 3am and the apartment is completely still except for us.

Brooke is next to me, legs tucked underneath her, phone in one hand, pen in the other because she keeps switching between typing and scribbling. We've got a notebook open between us. The 30-day challenge — Day 1. Rhyming words. That's it. That's the whole assignment.

And I'm *flying.*

Not in some dramatic way. Not like a breakthrough montage. Flying like — I say "frame" and before she even looks up I've already rattled off "claim, name, game, blame, shame, flame, tame, came, same, lane — wait, that doesn't —" and she's laughing because I overcorrected and I'm laughing because of course I did, and Teddy lifts his head from the arm of the couch like *can you two shut up* and that makes us laugh harder.

This is it. This is the thing.

Not a stadium. Not a studio session with a famous producer. Not a sync deal or a placement or a moment that looks good on a timeline. Just me and Brooke on the couch at 3am, trading words back and forth like we're playing catch, and everything about it is so simple it almost feels illegal.

---

## The Discovery

Here's what I didn't expect: I'm *good at this.*

Not good like I studied it. Good like I always was and just never aimed it at the right thing. All those years of wordplay — the jokes, the one-liners, the way I flip a phrase in conversation without thinking about it, the way I hear the rhythm inside a sentence before I hear the meaning — that wasn't a party trick. That was the instrument. I just didn't know I was practicing.

Brooke knew. She said it before I believed it: *You'd be a great songwriter.* Not "you could be" — you *would* be. Past tense confidence in a future thing. She saw the door before I even saw the hallway.

And now we're sitting here, and a rhyme scheme turns into a hook, and a hook turns into a verse, and she says "wait, go back to that line" and I go back and it's better the second time because she heard something in it I almost threw away. That's what it is to write with someone who loves you. They catch the things you'd drop.

---

## The Container

Day 1 of 30. That's all this is. A container.

Thirty days. One exercise a day. Small enough to be stupid. Simple enough to actually do. And inside that container — inside the constraint — everything opens up. The container creates freedom. I've always known this. I just never pointed it at songwriting before.

I'm not trying to write a masterpiece by Friday. I'm not calculating how this connects to the bigger picture. I'm doing Day 1. Tomorrow I'll do Day 2. The rhymes will become lines. The lines will become verses. The verses will become songs. And none of that has to happen tonight. Tonight is just rhyming words with my favorite person at 3am and feeling the gears click into a groove I didn't know I had.

The importance is zero. And that's exactly why it's working.

---

## The Feeling

I want to be specific about what this feels like, because this is the part I want to remember.

It feels like conversation. Not *work.* Brooke says a word and I riff on it and she riffs on my riff and suddenly we're four layers deep in something that sounds like a chorus and neither of us planned it. It just showed up because we were light enough to let it.

It feels like late nights used to feel when we first started dating — that buzzy, electric thing where you're tired but you don't want to stop because you're discovering something together. Except now the thing we're discovering isn't each other. It's this. This creative channel between us that was always there, just quiet.

It feels like Boo walking across the notebook and me writing around her paw print. It feels like Chicago outside the window, the lake out there somewhere in the dark, the city asleep and us awake making something that didn't exist an hour ago.

It feels like the Rancho Mirage studio already. Not the room — the *feeling.* That feeling of easy creative partnership, the desert sky outside, the two of us at a desk with monitors and instruments and space — that's not a destination. That's an expansion of what's already here. The feeling doesn't start when we get there. The feeling started tonight.

---

## The Proof

An hour ago I was making beats. A visualization told me to sit down and open FL Studio and let it be easy, and I did, and it was. The door opened and I walked through it.

Now I'm sitting on the other side of that door and there's *another* door. And this one's open too.

Making music was the first door. Writing songs with Brooke is the second. And walking through the second one was even easier than the first because I already remembered how it works: you stop making it heavy. You let it be play. You trust that the skill was there all along, hiding inside all the things you already do without trying.

Rhyming comes naturally. It always has. I just never gave myself credit for it because it felt too easy, and I'd been taught that valuable things are supposed to be hard. They're not. The easy thing, the natural thing, the thing that flows — that's the current. That's the path. That's the door.

---

## The Anchor

I look at Brooke. She's chewing on her pen cap, staring at the notebook, about to say something. This is the image I keep.

Not a future image. Not a someday image. *This* image. Right now. 3am. The notebook. The laughter still hanging in the air. The cats. The quiet apartment. The absolute absence of pressure.

I press my fingertips together — thumb to middle finger, both hands. Three seconds. I feel the ease in my chest. I hear the word she's about to say. I feel the next rhyme already forming before she finishes.

This is the anchor. When the grip comes — when I start making it heavy, when importance creeps in, when the pendulum says *but what if you're not actually good at this* — I press my fingers together and I'm back here. On the couch. With her. Words bouncing between us like we invented the game.

I'm writing songs with Brooke and it's simple. It's effective. It's us living our very best dream.

And it already started.
