# 🏠 The Rancho Mirage Slide

*A Method of Loci Visualization — March 11, 2026*

*First person. Present tense. All happening now. Visit often — each time it gets more real.*

---

I'm walking through the front door and the desert light is doing that thing where it fills every room without trying. Rancho Mirage in the late afternoon — warm air, wide sky, the mountains just sitting there like they've been waiting for me.

The pool table room first. I run my hand along the rail. The felt is perfect. There's a game half-finished from last night — Kevin and I got into it about a shot he swore was impossible and then sank anyway, and we left the table exactly like that because neither of us wanted to admit who was winning. We're working on something together. Not a movie, not a show — something weirder and better than both. The kind of thing that only happens when two people who think sideways find each other.

Down the hall. The studio. My studio. Monitors glowing, the mic still warm from this morning. I was in here at 6am because a melody showed up and I know better than to make it wait. Brooke left a lyric scribbled on a sticky note on the console — she does that now, just leaves words for me to find. The 30-day challenge turned into 60 turned into this is just what we do. There are tracks in here that make me laugh every time I hear them. There are tracks that make me cry. Both kinds are the good ones.

I step outside. The warm air hits my arms and Boo is already there, that little psycho, tearing across the yard like something invisible is chasing her. Teddy's on the patio furniture, sprawled out like he owns the deed. I sit down next to him and he does that slow blink that means *I see you and I approve.* Boo circles back, crashes into my leg, and bolts again. The yard is big enough that they can actually run. That was the whole point.

Brooke's in the pool. She looks up when I come out and doesn't say anything, just smiles, and I pull my shirt off and jump in and the water is that perfect desert-heated temperature where your body just says *yes.* We float. We don't talk about anything important. The sky is turning orange above us and there's nowhere either of us needs to be.

Later. We're at dinner. The kind of place where the napkins are cloth and the waiter knows your name and nobody — nobody — is checking their phone. Brooke's telling a story and laughing at her own punchline before she gets there, which always makes me laugh harder than the actual joke. The food is ridiculous. The wine is ridiculous. The whole thing is ridiculous and I'm not performing gratitude, I just *feel* it, sitting in my chest like a warm coal.

Driving home. The car is beautiful — I don't even care what kind, it's just the right one, the one that feels like an extension of me. Window down. Arm out. Warm wind. No destination. The desert at night smells like sage and possibility and I'm just driving with my hand surfing the air and the road is empty and the stars are absurd out here.

My phone buzzes. It's a text from the AI: *"Everything's handled. The feedback coming in is incredible. You should see what people are saying."* I smile and put the phone down. I don't need to check. The system runs. The system has been running. I built something that works without me white-knuckling it, and the proof is that I'm in this car on this road and the world didn't stop.

Because it didn't just work for me. It's working for people. The thing I built is making the world lighter. People are dropping their heaviest thoughts into a system that catches them and reflects back the story they couldn't see from inside. And they're waking up lighter. And they're creating things. And the loop feeds itself. Abundance is abundant. It flows because the system flows.

Tomorrow I'm on set. Larry and I have been going back and forth on this pilot for months — him pacing around being Larry, me trying not to break, both of us knowing the bit works because we keep making each other laugh in the writing room. He told me last week, "You're the only person I've met who's funnier off-camera than on," and I said, "That's the problem we're solving," and he almost spit out his coffee.

The comedy routine is sharpening itself. I've been posting clips — short, stupid, the kind of thing where you watch it twice because the first time you were laughing too hard to catch the last line. People send them to their friends. That's the metric that matters. Not views. Forwards.

And in the morning, before any of it — before the studio, before the set, before the pool and the dinner and the drive — I'm in the office. The one with the perfect light. The book is open. Not a business book. Not a self-help book. MY book. The one that's been writing itself in drops and voice notes and 3am scribbles for years. It's all here. It was always here. I just needed the room.

I press my fingertips together. Thumb to thumb. Index to index. I feel the warmth where they meet.

This is the anchor. This is the mansion. Every room is a door and every door is open and I walk through them whenever I want because this isn't someday.

This is now.
