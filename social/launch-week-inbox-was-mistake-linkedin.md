# Launch Week — The Inbox Was a Mistake

We need to talk about email.

Not the spam. Not the newsletters you never read. The *good* emails. The ones that matter.

They still bury you.

I spent years treating my inbox like a todo list. Every message was a task I hadn't done yet. The red badge was a scoreboard, and I was losing.

Here's what nobody tells you: the inbox was designed for *senders*, not receivers. It optimizes for "did they open it" not "did it help them."

So I stopped playing.

I built something that works the way my brain works. Drop anything — email, voice, screenshot, text. The system extracts what matters, connects it to what you already know, and delivers insights when you actually need them.

No folders. No tags. No "inbox zero" guilt.

Just: have the thought, drop it anywhere, get the insight later.

Your second brain has no inbox. It has a vault. And the vault doesn't judge you for having 847 unread items. It just finds the good stuff.
