## Final Post

I almost didn't build DropAnywhere.

Not because it was hard. Because it was too simple.

"Just email yourself thoughts and get them back organized?"

That felt like cheating. Real productivity tools have dashboards. Workflows. Integrations. At least a few toggle switches.

I kept adding complexity because simple felt embarrassing. Like showing up to a car race on a bicycle. Technically it moves, but where's the engine?

Then I noticed something.

Every time I made it more complex, people used it less. Every "feature" became friction. Every option became a decision. Every dashboard became another tab they'd never open.

The version people actually loved? Email in. Digest out. That's it.

I fought this for months. Added a web app. Built a capture widget. Created folders, tags, a whole organizational system.

Usage dropped each time.

So I deleted it all. Stripped it back to the thing that actually worked. Felt naked for about a week. Like I'd launched half a product.

Then the messages started coming in:

"I actually use this every day."

"First tool that doesn't feel like work."

"I forget I'm even using it — and that's the point."

Here's what I learned: We don't resist simple solutions because they're ineffective. We resist them because they don't justify our struggle.

If the answer is easy, what was all that suffering for?

But the suffering was the bug, not the feature.

Your second brain shouldn't feel like a second job. It should feel like... nothing. Just thoughts, captured, connected, returned when you need them.

No friction. No features. No dashboards.

Just thinking made easier.

→ drop-anywhere.com
