## The Post

Three years ago I lost a business idea in the shower.

I know how that sounds.

But I had genuinely convinced myself: I'll remember this. I don't need to write it down. This one is different.

I did not remember it.

I rebuilt 12 note-taking systems trying to solve the problem. Notion, Obsidian, paper notebooks, voice memos, a whiteboard in the bathroom (seriously).

Each one worked for about two weeks.

Then life happened. The system required effort I didn't have. Ideas came at inconvenient times. The folder structure that made sense in March made no sense by May.

I kept losing things.

The real problem wasn't my apps. It was me trying to organize my brain from the outside.

Your brain doesn't work in folders. It works in connections, associations, random sparks at 2am.

So I stopped trying to build a system.

I built DropAnywhere instead.

Send it anywhere. Get your insights tomorrow.

No organizing. No perfect system. No pretending your brain works in folders.

Just thinking — and a morning digest that does the connecting for you.

It took losing that shower idea three years ago to build something I actually use.

Hopefully you won't have to wait as long.

→ drop-anywhere.com

## Strategy Notes

**Purpose:** Human origin story. Makes the product feel earned, not engineered. Creates emotional investment.

**Key insight:** The "shower idea" opener is universally relatable. Everyone's lost a great idea. The loss is the hook.

**Voice:** Honest, slightly self-deprecating, warm. Not hustle-culture. Not fake vulnerable. Real.

**Structure:** Loss → repeated failure → insight → solution → invitation

**No woo-woo check:**
- ✅ Specific (12 systems, 3 years, whiteboard in bathroom)
- ✅ Honest about failure (not inspirational-poster failure)
- ✅ Clear cause → product (problem → built solution)
- ✅ No "journey" or "transformation" language

**Emotional arc:** Relatable loss → shared frustration → insight → hope → action

**CTA:** Soft close ("hopefully you won't wait as long") then hard link. Earns both.
