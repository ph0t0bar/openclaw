# Stop Rowing Upstream — LinkedIn Post
*Polished by ContentBot — March 17, 2026*

---

**I learned this the hard way: your best work doesn't come from grinding harder.**

Three years ago, I was pulling 14-hour days building my first product. Obsessing over every feature. Checking metrics every 20 minutes. "If it's not painful, I'm not doing it right."

Result? Burned out completely. The product flopped.

Last year, I built DropAnywhere in half the time with 1/3 the stress. Know what changed?

I stopped rowing upstream.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about building something meaningful:

**When you're forcing it, everything becomes twice as hard.**

Forced conversations don't convert. Forced features feel clunky. Forced marketing sounds desperate.

But when you're aligned with what you're building? The work gets easier, not harder.

Your best ideas come in the shower. Code flows without bugs. The right people find you at the right time.

This isn't magical thinking. It's pattern recognition.

**The test I use now:**

Before any decision, I ask: "Does this make me feel heavier or lighter?"

Heavy = obligation, "should," someone else's playbook
Light = curiosity, excitement, my gut saying "yes"

I follow the light. Every time.

**Real example:** I spent months trying to build a complex workflow system because that's what "serious" productivity apps do. It felt like pushing a boulder uphill.

Then I had a simple insight: what if people could just email themselves thoughts and get them back organized tomorrow?

That insight became DropAnywhere's core feature. Built in two weeks. Users love it.

The difference? I stopped fighting the current and started swimming with it.

**Your work doesn't have to be a struggle.**

Find the thing that feels obvious to you but impossible to everyone else. That's your current.

Then drop the oars and let it carry you.
