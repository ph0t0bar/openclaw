## The Post

Your biggest business problem might not be a business problem at all.

I've been tracking a pattern across 4 years of building companies.

Every time I hit a wall, I thought I needed a better strategy. A new hire. More runway. Different positioning.

But when I looked closer:

• Revenue stalls when I'm stalling on decisions
• Team friction spikes when I'm avoiding hard conversations  
• Everything feels heavy when I'm forcing instead of flowing

The external world is a mirror of internal state.

This isn't mystical thinking — it's practical physics. When you shift your mindset, when you untangle that internal knot, the external world has no choice but to reshuffle around the new signal you're putting out.

The wrong question: "What's broken in my business?"

The right question: "What am I projecting onto this situation?"

I used to think successful founders had it all figured out. Now I think they just learned to stop fighting themselves.

When you're aligned internally, the path forward becomes obvious. When you're fighting an internal battle, everything else becomes a battlefield too.

Success doesn't have to be a struggle. Flow is available.

But first, you have to look in the mirror.
